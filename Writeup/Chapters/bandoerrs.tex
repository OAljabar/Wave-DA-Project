This chapter contains a discussion of the background and observation errors in our DA system. Though the background are determined directly from the ensemble when using the ETKF method, unserstanding the form they take is crucial to understanding how the DA system works. This is because, according to equation \ref{eqn:analysisupdate}, the DA increments always lie in the column space of the background error covariance matrix. This means that the background errors determine how information from the observations can be propagated around the domain by DA. The observation errors are also important to understanding a DA system since the balance between observation and background errors determines how much weight is given to the observations by the DA. The observation errors must be estimated manually, and a description of the methods used to do this are included here.

\section{Diagnosis of Observation Errors}

To simplify the computation, we assume that the observation error covariance matrix $\mathbf{R}$ is diagonal, essnetially ignoring covariances between observations taken at different times. This also makes determining $\mathbf{R}$ easier since all that is required is to estimate the observation error variance at each point. To determine the magnitude of the observation errors, several methods are available. A simple one is to plot a histogram of observation - ensemble mean, called an $o - b$ plot. The spread in $o - b$ comes from a combination of observationa and background error, so a simple way of estimating the observation error is to assume that observation error accounts for a constant proportion of this error, say 50\%. An $o - b$ histogram for signficant wave height is shown in figure \ref{fig:hsominusb}. From the plot we see that the spread on the histogram is approximately 1m, so the standard deviation of observational errors in significant wave height may be estimated to be approximately 50cm.

Another thing that is visible from the $o-b$ plot is a bias in either the Hs forecasts or observations. The peak of the histogram is centred at around 30cm, which means that the forecast model tends to predict wave heights that are about 30cm less than those observed. If this bias is a result of the model, it would be desirable to investigate the capability of DA to reduce this bias. However, with the twin experiment method that is used in this project, this is not possible (see section \ref{sec:twinexp} for details).

The histogram approach may be quantified more precisely following \cite{Desroziers2006}, by defining $d^o_b = \y - \mathbf{H}\x_b$ and using the relation

\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\left[d^o_b\left( d^o_b \right)^\top \right] = \mathbf{H} \mathbf{P}^f \mathbf{H}^\top + \mathbf{R}
\end{equation}

Taking traces and dividing by the number of observations, we get a good estimate for the observation error variance which is

\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[ \sum_{i} \left( d^o_b \right)_i^2 \right]}{\text{\# observations}} - \frac{\text{Tr}\left( \mathbf{H} \mathbf{P}^f \mathbf{H}^\top \right)}{\text{\# observations}}
\label{eqn:obserrestimate}
\end{equation}

In our case, the observation operator is very simple and simply selects the forecast values from the appropriate location and times, so the diagonal elements of $H P^f H^\top$ are simply the ensemble forecast variances at the observation location and times.

Another available method is that used in \cite{Houghton2023}. They used a colocation study to estimate the error in wave height observations from a distributed network of drifting sofar spotter buoys. Pairs of simultaneous observations from locations within 50km of each other were collected and binned according to the average wave height of each pair. The difference of each pair was taken, and the standard deviation of of differences from each bin was computed. They found a strong dependence of error on wave height.

In our case, we only have observations from one buoy and they are available hourly, so we take the pairs to be observations one hour apart. We then calculate the standard deviation using the method described above to obtain the plot in figure \ref{fig:hsobsstd}. As in \cite{Houghton2023}, we find a strong linear relationship between errors and wave height, with the error standard deviation being approximately 9.6\% of the wave height.

Recall from figure \ref{fig:hsensseries} that the ensemble variance Hs peaks just before the peak in Hs and drops back to lower values as Hs is at its highest value. Since ensemble variance is a good measure of the magnitude of the background error, the background error is large compared to observational error just before the peak. This means that observations from this period will be given higher weighting than most. During and shortly after the peak, the opposite is true: background error is small compared to observational error, so observations from this period will be given lower weighting.

Repeating this process with the observations of Tm01 and Tm02 yields the plots shown in figure \ref{fig:periodobsstd}. The error distributions for Tm01 and Tm02 also fall aproximately on straight lines, though the fit is not as good as for Hs. The gradients are also shallower, with the error standard deviations being approximately 3.5\% of the observed variable for both. So we will model the error standard deviation in Hs as being equal to 10\% of Hs, and the error standard deviation in Tm01 and Tm02 as 3.5\% of Tm01 and Tm02 respectively. 

Since the errors in period are such a small percentage of the observations, it is possible that at low periods the error may become very small, which would lead the to DA system fitting the observations too closely and produing physically unrealistic increments. To avoid this, we set a minimum error threshold $\varepsilon_0$ and set the observation error standard deviation equal to this threshold whenever the scaling method gives a value less than $\varepsilon_0$. This threshold is determined using the method from \cite{Desroziers2006}, using only the observations of wave height with a value of less than 5 seconds, as these are the observations which may lead to small errors.

The terms in \ref{eqn:obserrestimate} were calculated to be

\[
\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[ \sum_{i} \left( d^o_b \right)_i^2 \right]}{\text{\# observations}} = 0.16 s^2, \frac{\text{Tr}\left( \mathbf{H} \mathbf{P}^f \mathbf{H}^\top \right)}{\text{\# observations}} = 0.11 s^2
\]

So the observation error variance is estimated to be $0.05 s^2$ and the standard deviation threshold is set at $0.23s$.
