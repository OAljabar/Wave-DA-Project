\section{The need for Reliable Wave Forecasting}

Offshore operations form an important part of many industries such as energy and research, and contribute to the economies of coastal countries round the world. Ocean waves pose a significant risk to offshore operations as they can lead to the loss of life and damage to equipment. This means that accurate wave predictions are a critical safety issue for offshore operations, as offshore operations can only be carried out safely when the waves are sufficiently calm. To provide these predictions the MetOcean Forecasting division of Fugro produces wave forecasts for a client undertaking offshore operations in the Gulf of Mexico. It is important to understand the risk posed by waves in this region since increased cyclone intensity is leading to increased extreme wave heights in the Gulf of Mexico \citep{Appendini2014}. To avoid unnecessary downtime, they produce ensemble forecasts and apply corrections using a proprietary method based on the alpha factor method described in \cite{Wu2021}. 

This proprietary method is referred to by Fugro as a site-specific alpha factor and seeks to correct for the bias and uncertainty present in the forecast by defining the alpha factor as follows:

\begin{equation}
\alpha = \frac{H_\text{max}}{H_\text{max,cor}}
\end{equation}

where $H_\text{max}$ is the maximum wave height with a $10^{-4}$ probability of exceedance during a given window. $H_\text{max,cor}$ is the maximum wave height with the same exceedance probability during the same window, after the forecast is corrected for bias and uncertainty. The Rayleigh distribution is used to model the probability distribution of maximum wave height over a window as follows: (cite)

\begin{equation}
\prob (H \leq H_\text{max}) = \left[ 1 - e^{\left( -2 \frac{H^2}{H_s^2} \right)} \right]^\frac{t}{T_p}
\label{eqn:rayleigh}
\end{equation}

Where $H_s$ is the significant wave height during the window, $t$ is the length of the window and $T_p$ is the mean period during the window. $H_\text{max}$ is then chosen so that $1 - \prob (H \leq H_\text{max}) = 10^{-4}$. $H_\text{max,cor}$ is found by integrating the joint probability density function of $H$ and $H_s$:

\begin{equation}
\prob (H,H_s) = \prob (H|H_s) \prob (H_s)
\end{equation}

Here $\prob (H|H_s)$ is the Rayleigh distribution from equation \ref{eqn:rayleigh} and $\prob (H_s)$ is assumed to be a normal distribution with mean equal to the bias corrected forecast value of $H_s$ and standard deviation equal to the forecast ensemble standard deviation. Integrating $\prob (H,H_s)$ over all values of $H_s$ gives a corrected distribution of maximum wave height during the window which can be used to define $H_\text{max,cor}$ in the same way as $H_\text{max}$.

Once the value of $\alpha$ is found, it is used to scale the $H_s$ model output via $H_{s,\text{cor}} = \frac{H_s}{\alpha}$. So if the model predicts $H_s = 1.5$m and $\alpha = 0.75$, the scaled forecast value is $H_{s,\text{cor}} = 2$m. From speaking to Fugro engineers we learned that this method is overly cautious and produces unrealistically high waves when $H_s$ is large.

Qualitatively speaking, $\alpha$ will always be less than 1, since forecast uncertainty acts to broaden the distribution of maximum wave height. This means that $H_\text{max,cor}$ is always more than $H\text{max}$, as illustrated in figure \ref{fig:Hcordist}

\section{Previous work on DA for Wave Forecasts}

Several studies have been completed previously investigating the effectiveness of DA methods to improve wave forecasts. In this section, some of these studies and their findings are summarised, and the differences between this study and pervious ones are discussed.

\cite{Caires2018} investigated the effect on wave forecasts of using an ensemble Kalman filter (EnKF) method to adjust the wind fields used as forcing in a wave model. They used hourly observations from six locations. Their results are shown in figure\ref{fig:caires2018fig30}.

An improvement is seen in forecasts of wave height and period, but it is relatively small compared to other studies and short lived, not extending past 24hrs in any instance (check). This may be due to the indirect relationship between the assimilated wind data and the forecasted wave variables. We hope that assimilating the forecasted variables directly will lead to improved performance over this wind-based approach.

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width = \textwidth]{figures/caires2018fig30}
\caption{RMSE in wave height (left) and period (right) from two different observation locations. Figure 30 from \cite{Caires2018}.}
\label{fig:caires2018fig30}
\end{figure}

Another possible reason for the relatively poor performance in this case is the small number of observations used. Observations were available hourly from only six different locations, while operational DA systems commonly assimilate upwards of $10^6$ observations at a time. This study is also limited in this regard, as data is only available from on buoy. A study which used many more observations is \cite{Houghton2022}, in which an optimal interpolation (OI) method (cite) was used to compare the effects of assimilating wave height and spectra. In this study, observations came from the global Sofar spotter buoy network, which contains over 600 buoys. A plot of their results is shown in figure \ref{fig:houghton2022fig3}.

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width = \textwidth]{Figures/houghton2022fig3}
\caption{RMSE from spectral DA and wave height (Hs) DA, normalised by the RMSE without DA. Results shown for significant wave height Hs, peak period $T_p$, mean period $T_m$, directional spread $\sigma_\theta$, peak direction $D_p$ and mean direction $D_m$. Figure 3 from \citet{Houghton2022}.}
\label{fig:houghton2022fig3}
\end{figure}

Figure \ref{fig:houghton2022fig3} shows that both variables give very similar improvements in predictions of wave height, while spectral assimilation gives better results for other quantities such as period and direction. They also found that the spectral DA did particularly well in swell-dominated fields when compared to assimilating wave height. This shows that the type of observations assimilated can have a large effect on the performance of a DA system.

In this project we assimilate two measures of period, Tm01 and Tm02. These variables are related to different moments of the spectrum (see equations \ref{eqn:moments} - \ref{eqn:mainvars}), so we hope that assimilating both Tm01 and Tm02 as well as wave height will have some of the benefits of spectral DA identified in \cite{Houghton2022}. Since assimilating a full wave spectrum involves much more data than bulk variables such as wave height and period, we hope also that our method will incur less computational cost than assimilating a full spectrum.

To investigate the effect of the DA method used, \cite{Houghton2023} assimilated wave height observations from satellite altimeters and Sofar spotter buoys. They compared two different DA methods, OI and the ensemble-based local ensemble transform Kalman filter (LETKF) \citep{Hunt2007}. A plot of their results is included in figure \ref{fig:houghton2023fig6}.

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width = .8\textwidth]{figures/houghton2023fig6}
\caption{Forecast bias and RMSE from altimeter observations (top) and buoy observations (bottom). Figure 6 from \cite{Houghton2023}.}
\label{fig:houghton2023fig6}

They found that both methods reduced forecast error and bias out to 2.5 days leadtime, with LETKF having slightly greater forecast skill overall. In addition to this, they found that the LETKF gave more physically realistic increments due to its flow dependent background error covariance matrix. This shows that the as well as the observations, the DA method used affects DA performance. In this project we use the ensemble transform Kalman filter (ETKF) method, which is related to the LETKF. In this project, we apply DA to a specific forecasting context and client in the Gulf of Mexico, and so we are constrained by the observation data available in this context. As seen above, the studies with more observations saw better DA performance, and our results should be evaluated with this limitation in mind. Another limitation which none of the previous studies were subject to is that we are unable to run the wave model ourselves, which is a central step of most DA systems. We hope that the knowledge gained from these previous studies will allow us to get good results in spite of these limitations.

\section{Aims of the Project}

The main research question in this project is ``\textbf{What kind of reduction in forecast error can be achieved by assimilating Hs, Tm01 and Tm02 using the ensemble transform Kalman filter method?}''

To this end, we perform a twin experiment using the ensemble transform Kalman filter (ETKF) to assimilate observations of the three variables. A twin experiment is a DA experiment in which fictional obersvations are gerenated and assimilated in place of real observations (see section \ref{sec:twinexp} for details). We perform one experiment in which all three aforementioned variables are assimilated, and one experiment in which wave height alone is assimilated. The reasoning for this is that wave height extremes are of particular interest to Fugro's client due to their impact on operational safety, so we consider $H_s$ to be the most important variable of the three. We examine the results of these two experments in comparison with each other, and with the forecast data before DA, to evaluate the performance of DA in both instances.

Various aspects of the results will be examined to gain a full understanding of the DA performance. These are:

\begin{itemize}
\item Increments. The changes made to the forecast by the DA system are called increments, and they can aid understanding of how DA is modifying the model state. If the DA system is performing well, we expect to see physically realistic increments.
\item The root mean square error (RMSE) of the ensemble mean. We expect the DA to move the ensemble mean closer to the true state so RMSE will be used to quantify and test this. The dependence of RMSE on forecast leadtime and location within the domain will be examined.
\item The correlation between ensemble mean and the true state. DA should make the ensemble mean more closely correlated with the true state, so we will test this hypothesis.
\item Ensemble spread. This is an important measure of uncertainty in ensemble forecasts, which allows a forecast to inform decisions with greater or lesser confidence. We hope that DA will reduce the ensemble spread, thus allowing decisions to be made with greater confidence based on the forecasts. The ensemble spread is compared to the RMSE to check for `overconfidence' in the post-DA ensemble (see section \ref{sec:DApractice}).
\item Errors in maximum Hs. Extremes of Hs are of particular interest in these forecasts as these are the events which cause danger and require the pausing of offshore operations. We will examine the effectiveness of DA to reduce error in the timings and magnitudes of wave height maxima.
\end{itemize}
