\section{The need for Reliable Wave Forecasting}

Offshore operations \replace{form an important of}{are common in} many industries such as energy and research, and contribute to the economies of coastal countries round the world. Ocean waves pose a significant risk to offshore operations as they can lead to the loss of life and damage to equipment. This means that accurate wave predictions are a critical safety issue for offshore operations, \replace{the the}{as offshore} operations can only be carried out safely when the waves are sufficiently calm. To provide these predictions the MetOcean Forecasting division of Fugro produces wave forecasts for a client undertaking offshore operations in the Gulf of Mexico. It is important to understand the risk posed by waves in this region since increased cyclone intensity is leading to increased extreme wave heights in the Gulf of Mexico \citep{Appendini2014}. To avoid unnecessary downtime, they produce ensemble forecasts and apply corrections using a proprietary method based on the alpha factor method described in \cite{Wu2021}. 

\highlight{This proprietary method is referred to by Fugro as a site-specific alpha factor and works by...}

As well as these forecasts, Fugro has a buoy based near to the client which produces hourly observations of the sea state. Data assimilation refers to a range of techniques used to integrate observations into a forecast, with the aim of making the forecast more accurate. In this project, we explore the potential of DA to reduce uncertainty and error in these wave forecasts.
