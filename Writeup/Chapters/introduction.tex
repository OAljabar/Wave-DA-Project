\section{The need for Reliable Wave Forecasting}

Offshore operations form an important part of many industries such as energy and research\remove{,} and contribute to the economies of coastal countries round the world. Ocean waves pose a significant risk to offshore operations as they can lead to the loss of life and damage to equipment. This means that accurate wave predictions are a critical safety issue for offshore operations, as offshore operations can only be carried out safely when the waves are sufficiently calm. To provide these predictions\add{,} the MetOcean Forecasting division of Fugro produces wave forecasts for a client undertaking offshore operations in the Gulf of Mexico. It is important to understand the risk posed by waves in this region since increased cyclone intensity is leading to increased extreme wave heights in the Gulf of Mexico \citep{Appendini2014}. To avoid unnecessary downtime, they produce ensemble forecasts and apply corrections using a proprietary method based on the alpha factor method described in \cite{Wu2021}. 

This proprietary method is referred to by Fugro as a site-specific alpha factor and seeks to correct for the bias and uncertainty present in the forecast by defining the alpha factor

\begin{equation}
\alpha = \frac{H_\text{max}}{H_\text{max,cor}},
\end{equation}

where $H_\text{max}$ is the \comment{maximum wave height with a}{It's not really a maximum then. I would just define it as the waveheight such that has this 10e-4 probability of exceedance} $10^{-4}$ probability of exceedance during a given window. $H_\text{max,cor}$ is the maximum wave height with the same exceedance probability during the same window, after the forecast is corrected for bias and uncertainty. Following \cite{Liu1998}, the Rayleigh distribution is used to model the probability distribution of maximum wave height over a window as follows:

\begin{equation}
\prob (H \leq H_\text{max}) = \left[ 1 - e^{\left( -2 \frac{H_\text{(max)}^2}{H_s^2} \right)} \right]^\frac{t}{T_p}.
\label{eqn:rayleigh}
\end{equation}

Here $H_s$ is the significant wave height during the window, $t$ is the length of the window and $T_p$ is the mean period during the window. $H_\text{max}$ is then chosen so that $1 - \prob (H \leq H_\text{max}) = 10^{-4}$. $H_\text{max,cor}$ is found by integrating the joint probability density function of $H$ and $H_s$,

\begin{equation}
\prob (H,H_s) = \prob (H|H_s) \prob (H_s),
\end{equation}

where $\prob (H|H_s)$ \comment{is}{I think that H should be H\_{max} here} the Rayleigh distribution from equation \ref{eqn:rayleigh} and $\prob (H_s)$ is assumed to be a normal distribution with mean equal to the bias corrected forecast value of $H_s$ and standard deviation equal to the forecast standard deviation. The bias and standard deviation were calculated from the timeseries of model output and observations spanning from September 2020 to May 2021. Integrating $\prob (H,H_s)$ over all values of $H_s$ gives a corrected distribution of maximum wave height during the window which can be used to define $H_\text{max,cor}$ in the same way as $H_\text{max}$.

Once the value of $\alpha$ is found, it is used to scale the $H_s$ model output via $H_{s,\text{cor}} = \frac{H_s}{\alpha}$. So if the model predicts $H_s = 1.5$m and $\alpha = 0.75$, the scaled forecast value is $H_{s,\text{cor}} = 2$m. Qualitatively speaking, $\alpha$ will always be less than 1, since forecast uncertainty acts to broaden the distribution of maximum wave height. This means that $H_\text{max,cor}$ is always more than $H_\text{max}$, as illustrated in figure \ref{fig:Hcordist}.

Fugro has conducted research into the performance of the site-specific alpha factor method, but has not made the report publicly available. However, from private correspondence with Fugro scientists \citep{Nikolova2024}, we learned that this method is overly cautious and systematically produces wave heights above those seen in the observational record. This conclusion was reached after examining the performance of the method in five strong storms between January and March 2022.

As well as producing these forecasts, Fugro has a buoy based near to the client which produces hourly observations of the sea state. Data assimilation \add{(DA)} refers to a range of techniques used to integrate observations into a forecast, with the aim of making the forecast more accurate. In this project, we explore the potential of DA to reduce uncertainty and error in these wave forecasts. We hope that DA will give better results than the alpha factor method currently in use.

\section{Aims of the Project}
\label{sec:aim}

The main research question in this project is ``\textbf{What kind of reduction in forecast error can be achieved by assimilating $H_s$, $T_{m01}$ and $T_{m02}$ using the ensemble transform Kalman filter method?}''

To this end, we perform a twin experiment using the ensemble transform Kalman filter (ETKF) to assimilate observations of the three variables. A twin experiment is a DA experiment in which fictional observations are generated and assimilated in place of real observations (see section \ref{sec:twinexp} for details). We perform one experiment in which all three aforementioned variables are assimilated, and one experiment in which wave height alone is assimilated. \remove{The reasoning for this is that} wave height extremes are of particular interest to Fugro's client due to their impact on operational safety, so we consider $H_s$ to be the most important variable of the three. We examine the results of these two experiments in comparison with each other, and with the forecast data before DA, to evaluate the performance of DA in both instances.

\begin{itemize}
\item Increments. The changes made to the forecast by the DA system are called increments, and they can aid understanding of how DA is modifying the model state. If the DA system is performing well, we expect to see physically realistic increments.
\item The root mean square error (RMSE) of the ensemble mean. We expect the DA to move the ensemble mean closer to the true state so RMSE will be used to quantify and test this. The dependence of RMSE on forecast leadtime and location within the domain will be examined.

\item Ensemble spread. This is an important measure of uncertainty in ensemble forecasts, which can give a good estimate of forecast error and allow a forecast to inform decisions with greater or lesser confidence. We hope that DA will reduce the ensemble spread, thus allowing decisions to be made with greater confidence based on the forecasts. The ensemble spread is compared to the RMSE to check for `overconfidence' in the post-DA ensemble (see section \ref{sec:DApractice}).
\item Errors in maximum $H_s$. Extremes of $H_s$ are of particular interest in these forecasts as these are the events which cause danger and require the pausing of offshore operations. We will examine the effectiveness of DA to reduce error in the timings and magnitudes of wave height maxima.
