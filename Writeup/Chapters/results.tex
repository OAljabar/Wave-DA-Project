
\chapter{Results}
\label{Chapter5} 

This chapter contains an analysis of the errors in the DA output as compared to the model output with no DA. We also examine the results of assimilating $H_s$ alone ($H_s$ DA) compared to assimilating $H_s$, $T_{m01}$ and $T_{m02}$ (all vars DA).

For the purposes of a simple visual examination, we include timeseries of ensemble mean from each run, plotted alongside the truth. Figure \ref{fig:buoymeanseries9} shows this for variables $H_s$ and $T_{m01}$ at the buoy location from the 12Z 09/03/2022 model run. We see little change in $H_s$ during the assimilation window as the no DA forecast matches the truth very closely here. After the end of the window, we see that $H_s$ DA matches truth very well, while all vars DA is close to no DA.

From the $T_{m01}$ plot, we see that $H_s$ is similar to no DA during the window while all vars DA is close to the truth. This is unsurprising as $T_{m01}$ is assimilated directly in all vars DA while $H_s$ is not. This means that any change to $T_{m01}$ from $H_s$ DA relies on cross covariance between the two variables, which is generally small. This indicates that the different variables being assimilated are having the effects we expect them to have. After the window, the performance is more variable: $H_s$ DA does well at 3-4 days, all runs match the truth closely between 4 and days, and none are very close after 5 days. The divergence at 3-4 days and after 5 days is to be expected as this is when the largest errors are present in the original forecasts: 3-4 days because of the large swell event and 5+ days because of the chaos inherent in wave forecasts.

For comparison, figure \ref{fig:farmeanseries9} shows the same timeseries at $30\degrees$ N  $80\degrees$ W, which is outside the GoM in the northern Atlantic ocean. As this location is so far from the buoy, we do not expect to see any kind of improvement from the DA, and indeed this is what we see. All vars DA, $H_s$ DA and no DA are all a similar distance from the truth which indicates that DA has not improved the forecast. One thing to note is that there is often a large difference between the no DA line and both the $H_s$ DA and all vars DA lines, meaning that the increments $\vect{d}^a_b = \x_a - \x_b$ are quite large. This is physically unrealistic as we would not expect the ocean state at the buoy location to have any effect on the ocean state at $30\degrees$ N  $80\degrees$ W. This is explored further in section \ref{sec:increments}.

The divergence of the different runs after the swell event tells us that the DA system has captured the dynamical chaos of the physical wave system.

\section{Increments}
\label{sec:increments}

The increments $\vect{d}^a_b = \x^a - \x_b$ are the corrections applied to the background state by the DA system. From equation \ref{eqn:cost3d}, the state that minimises the cost function will be close to the background when $\mat{P}^f$ is small compared to $\mat{R}$. This means that we expect the increments to be small in this situation. We have seen that generally $\mat{P}^f$ is small at locations far from the buoy, with some ecxeptions noted.% (and at times far from the assimilation window,)

From equation \ref{eqn:analysisupdate}, the increments are a linear function of the innovations $\vect{d}^o_b = \y - \mat{H}\x_b$. We expect the increments and the innovations to have the same sign, so that the DA moves the analysis state closer to the truth than the forecast state.

Figure \ref{fig:hsinc} shows the increments in $H_s$ ensemble mean at 12Z 12/03/2022 taken from the 12Z 10/03/2022 forecast run. We see that all vars DA has larger increments overall, and this is especially true far from the buoy. The increments from both DA runs match the sign of the innovations somewhat well, but both have regions where the sign is incorrect. A notable example is the strong positive increment from all vars DA in the Northern GoM, where the innovation is slightly negative. The lower magnitude of increments in $H_s$ DA means it undercorrects in some regions where the magnitude of innovations is large, such as just Northwest of the buoy in the Western GoM. All vars DA does a better job at correcting for this large innovation.

Overall, all vars DA does better than $H_s$ DA at predicting both $H_s$ and $T_{m01}$ near the buoy after the assimilation window, while both perform similarly during the window. The improvements seen during the window are more widepsread in $H_s$ DA, while after the end of the window the opposite is true. All vars DA shows larger changes in errors in general, and particularly at locations away from the buoy, which is in line with the larger increments seen in all vars DA.

\section{Ensemble Spread}

Ensemble spread is an important measure of uncertianty in ensemble forecasts. One aim of this project is to reduce uncertainty in the forecasts so that they may be used to inform decisions with greater confidence. Figure \ref{fig:DAensspread} shows timeseries of ensemble standard deviation in $H_s$ and $T_{m01}$ at the buoy location from two different model runs. Both $H_s$ DA and all vars DA show a reduction in ensemble spread in both variables, as is expected from DA (section \ref{sec:DApractice}).

All vars DA shows a greater reduction in $T_{m01}$ ensemble spread overall. This is unsurprising since $T_{m01}$ is not directly assimilated in $H_s$ DA. This means that any increment to $T_{m01}$ from $H_s$ DA comes from cross covariances between $H_s$ and $T_{m01}$, which we have seen are small (figure \ref{fig:covhst0112-0}), so we expect the overall effect of $H_s$ DA on $T_{m01}$ to be smaller than the effect of all vars DA. In $H_s$ ensemble spread, both runs perform similarly but $H_s$ DA generally shows lower spread during the assimilation window and the opposite is true after the assimilation window. This is likely because the observational error scale in $H_s$ was increased from $10\%$ in $H_s$ DA to $12\%$ in all vars DA.

At both leadtimes, All vars DA shows a greater reduction in ensemble spread than $H_s$ DA across most of the domain. The only exception to this is the vicinity of the buoy at 1 day leadtime, when $H_s$ DA shows a greater reduction. This is similar to what we saw in the timeseries, when $H_s$ DA had greater spread reduction outside of the assimilation window. This reflects the fact that the entire 7-day forecast window is updated in one step by the DA system, so the dimensions of space and time within a forecast window are treated equivalently. As a result of this, is it not surprising to see parallels in the spatial and temporal distributions of ensemble spread. The greatest reductions in spread are where the spread of no DA is greatest.

Figure \ref{fig:t01spreadmaps} shows the same as \ref{fig:hsspreadmaps} but for $T_{m01}$. At 5 days leadtime, the reduction from all vars DA generally matches the pattern of standard deviation from no DA, with a peak concentrated on the western side of the Yucatan peninsula. This peak is also present in $H_s$ DA, which shows little reduction in spread elsewhere.

Overall, all vars DA shows greater reduction in ensemble spread at most times and locations, except near the buoy during the assimilation window. The reductions in spread are greatest where the no DA ensemble spread is greatest, which is good as this reduces the maximum uncertainty in the forecast.

It is not desirable to focus solely on reducing the ensemble spread, as this may lead to the ensemble being overconfident. As described in section \ref{sec:DApractice}, it is detrimental to the forecast to have ensemble spread smaller than the forecast errors as this can lead to too much confidence being placed in an inaccurate forecast. To test whether this problem has arisen, we plot the ensemble standard deviation against the RMSE.

Figure \ref{fig:hsspreaderr} shows a scatter plot of ensemble mean against RMSE at the buoy location. RMSE is calculated from the ensemble mean and averaged over a 6 hour rolling window in leadtime. Data from all forecast windows from $H_s$ DA and all vars DA is included, with $H_s$ DA represented by circles and all vars DA represented by triangles. The leadtime of each data point within its forecast window is shown by its colour. Lines of best fit are calculated for both $H_s$ DA and all vars DA, and plotted along with the 1:1 line.

We see from the lines of best fit that both DA runs have RMSE generally below the ensemble spread, with all vars DA being lower than $H_s$ DA overall. Data points above the 1:1 line correspond to overconfident ensembles, so for both variables from both DA runs, the number of data points above the line was counted and shown in table \ref{tab:conffreq}. The equivalent counts from no DA are also included. The total number of data points in each run is 984, so we see that all vars DA gives overconfident $H_s$ ensembles about 22\% of the time and overconfident $T_{m01}$ ensembles about 30\% of the time. $H_s$ DA gives overconfident $H_s$ ensembles about 30\% of the time and overconfident $T_{m01}$ ensembles about 25\% of the time. The colours on the plot show that most of the occasions where RMSE is significantly more than spread come late in the forecast window (leadtime > 4 days) with few exceptions.

So $H_s$ DA gives more overconfident $H_s$ ensembles and all vars DA gives more overconfident $T_{m01}$ ensembles, with $H_s$ DA giving more overconfident ensembles overall. The high prevalence of overconfident $T_{m01}$ ensembles from all vars DA lines up with the decreased spread in $T_{m01}$ from all vars DA seen in figure \ref{fig:DAensspread}, and may be due to the increased reduction in ensemble spread from multiple variables being assimilated. %I was not able to find an explanation for the high prevalence of overconfident $H_s$ ensembles from $H_s$ DA, and further work is needed to understand this. (change)

Overconfident ensembles are most important to avoid when the error is large, since larger forecast errors present greater risk for incorrect decision making. So we repeat the count of the overconfident ensembles and apply a minimum threshold that the RMSE must meet in order for the ensemble to be counted. These thresholds are 0.1 m for $H_s$ and 0.2 s for $T_{m01}$, and were set at values that we judge to be acceptable levels of error in each variable. After applying these thresholds, the frequency of overconfident ensembles reduced by at least 50\% in all cases except the $T_{m01}$ ensembles from $H_s$ DA. This reflects the fact that the errors in $T_{m01}$ from $H_s$ DA are large. This now gives overconfidence prevalences of 10-20\% in most cases.

\section{Errors in Maximum $H_s$}

$H_s$ maxima are of particular interest in this project as they present the most danger to offshore operations. In this section we examine the effect of DA on the errors in the timing and magnitude of the $H_s$ maximum on 12/03/2022. Figure \ref{fig:peakerrors} shows scatter plots of the timing and magnitude from no DA, $H_s$ DA and all vars DA, relative to the truth. The maximum is selected from the period spanning 12Z 12/03/2022 to 12Z 13/03/2022. The left plot is generated from the model run starting 12Z 10/03/2022 and the right plot is generated from the model run starting 12Z 08/03/2022, giving leadtime ranges of 2-3 days and 4-5 days respectively. Table \ref{tab:hsmax} shows the ensemble RMSE in timing and magnitude from each ensemble for both runs.

Note on the scatter plots that the points are arranged discretely at integer numbers of hours: this is simply beacuse the forecast data used has a time resolution of one hour. This could reduce the maximum wave height detected if the true maximum lies in the middle of two timesteps. One way around this may be to fit a quadratic polynomial to the model data points either side of the peak and find the maximum value of this polynomial, but this was not explored here. The forecasters at Fugro work regularly with hourly forecast data without any interpolation applied, and we assume they are aware that this can lead to inaccurate maximum values being sampled.

With a leadtime of 2-3 days, we see a large improvement in forecasts of timing and magnitude from both DA runs. From the scatter plot, the ensemble spread is considerably tighter around the true value, and the RMSE in both quantities is reduced. All vars DA does slightly better than $H_s$ DA in predicting the timing of the maximum, and vice versa for the magnitude, but the differences between the two are small compared to the difference of both with no DA.

After 4-5 days, the errors are larger in all quantities but the magnitude from the no DA run. The differences between the two DA runs is larger, as $H_s$ DA has a similar error in timing to no DA, but shows a small improvement in the magnitude of 13\%. Conversely, all vars DA has similar error in magnitude to no DA, but shows an improvement in timing of 35\%.

Overall, $H_s$ DA and all vars DA perform similarly to each other during the assimilation window, and both significantly outperform  no DA as we expect during this time. As seen in other aspects, their performance differs more after the assimilation window, with $H_s$ DA providing better forecasts of the magnitude of the peak and all vars DA providing better forecasts of its timing.
