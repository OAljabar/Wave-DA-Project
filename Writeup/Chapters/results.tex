This chapter contains an analysis of the errors in the DA output as compared to the model output with no DA. We also examine the results of assimilating Hs alone (Hs DA) compared to assimilating Hs, Tm01 and Tm02 (all vars DA).

For the purposes of a simple visual examination, we include timeseries of ensemble mean from each run, plotted alongside the truth. Figure \ref{fig:buoymeanseries9} shows this for variables Hs and Tm01 at the buoy location from the 12Z 09/03/2022 model run. We see little change in Hs during the assimilation window as the no DA forecast matches the truth very closely here. After the end of the window, we see that Hs DA matches truth very well, while all vars DA is close to no DA. From the Tm01 plot, we see that Hs is similar to no DA during the window while all vars DA is close to the truth. After the window, the performance is more variable: Hs DA does well between 3 and 4 days, all runs match the truth closely between 4 and days, and none are very close after 5 days. (steps in no DA at 5-6 days? Ask Irina)

For comparison, figure \ref{fig:farmeanseries9} shows the same timeseries at $30\degrees$ N  $80\degrees$ W, which is outside the GoM in the northern Atlantic ocean. As this location is so far from the buoy, we do not expect to see any kind of improvement from the DA, and indeed this is what we see. All vars DA, Hs DA and no DA are all a similar distance from the truth which indicates that DA has not improved the forecast. One thing to note is that there is often a large difference between the no DA line and both the Hs DA and all vars DA lines, meaning that the increments $\vect{d}^a_b = \x_a - \x_b$ are quite large. This is physically unrealistic as we would not expect the ocean state at the buoy location to have any effect on the ocean state at $30\degrees$ N  $80\degrees$ W. This is explored further in section \ref{sec:increments}.

\section{Ensemble Spread}

Ensemble spread is an important measure of uncertianty in ensemble forecasts. One aim of this project is to reduce uncertainty in the forecasts so that they may be used to inform decisions with greater confidence. Figure \ref{fig:DAensspread} shows the ensemble standard deviation in Hs and Tm01 at the buoy location from two different model runs. Both Hs DA and all vars DA show a reduction in ensemble spread in both variables. All vars DA shows a greater reduction in Tm01 ensemble spread overall. This is unsurprising since Tm01 is not directly assimilated in Hs DA. This means that any increment to Tm01 from Hs DA comes from cross covariances between Hs and Tm01, which we generally expect to be small. In Hs ensemble spread, both runs perform similarly but Hs DA generally shows lower spread during the assimilation window and the opposite is true after the assimilation window. This is likely because the observational error scale in Hs was increased from $10\%$ in Hs DA to $12\%$ in all vars DA.

\section{Increments}
\label{sec:increments}

The increments $\vect{d}^a_b = \x_a - \x_b$ are the corrections made to the background state by the DA system. From equation \ref{eqn:cost3d}, the state that minimises the cost function will be close to the background when $\mat{B}$ is small compared to $\mat{R}$. This means that we expect the increments to be small in this situation. In general, we expect $\mat{B}$ to be small at locations far from the buoy and at times far from the assimilation window, since the covariance between ocean state at two points should generally decrease with increased distance/time separation.

Figure \ref{fig:hsinc} shows the increments in Hs at 12Z 15/03/2022 taken from the 12Z 10/03/2022 forecast run. Increments are shown both for a randomly selected ensemble member and for the ensemble mean. We see that all vars DA has large increments far from the buoy location, which is physically unrealistic as described above. This could be due to the observation error being set too small, as this would lead the analysis state to deviate too far from the background in order to match the observations. This could be fixed with further refinement of the observation error scale. It could also be due to spurious long-distance covariances in $\mat{P}^f$, which might arise due to sampling error when calculating $\mat{P}^f$. This could be fixed with localisation.
